A less-famous probability game

Can your coin pattern be beaten?

In Penney’s game, two three-flip patterns race to appear first in a stream of fair coin tosses. The strange part: after you choose, a second player can always choose a pattern with an advantage.

Read about Penney’s game
Choose your sequence

Counter-pattern—

Pick any three-flip pattern.

What is the trick?

The counter-pattern uses overlap. If your pattern is ABC, a strong reply is the opposite of B followed by AB. That makes the opponent’s pattern more likely to complete first, even though every individual toss is still fair.

What happens inside the model?

Each day, each path draws an independent standard normal shock z. The model updates the price using geometric Brownian motion: S(t + Δt) = S(t) × exp[(μ − σ²/2)Δt + σ√Δt × z], with Δt = 1/252. The simulation treats 252 trading days as one year, excluding weekends and market holidays.

Drift and volatility stay constant. This demonstration does not include market regimes, jumps, fees, or fundamentals. The paths illustrate uncertainty; they are not forecasts or investment recommendations.
